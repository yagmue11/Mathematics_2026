### Exercise 7. Classifying a Second-Degree Equation

Determine the type of object described by:

1. $4x^2+9y^2=36$,
2. $x^2-y^2=1$,
3. $x^2-4y=0$,
4. $x^2+y^2+1=0$.

In the last case, explain why there are no real points.

### Solution

**1. $4x^2 + 9y^2 = 36$**

Dividing both sides by $36$ gives

$$
\frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1.
$$

This is an **ellipse** centered at $(0,0)$ with semiaxes $a = 3$ and $b = 2$.

**2. $x^2 - y^2 = 1$**

Writing this as

$$
\frac{x^2}{1^2} - \frac{y^2}{1^2} = 1,
$$

this is an **equilateral (rectangular) hyperbola** centered at $(0,0)$ with vertices at $(\pm 1, 0)$ and asymptotes $y = \pm x$.

**3. $x^2 - 4y = 0$**

Rewriting the equation as

$$
x^2 = 4y,
$$

this is a **parabola** with vertex at $(0,0)$, axis of symmetry along the $y$-axis ($x = 0$), opening upward.

**4. $x^2 + y^2 + 1 = 0$**

This describes the **empty set** (an imaginary circle with no real points). For any real numbers $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$, their squares satisfy $x^2 \ge 0$ and $y^2 \ge 0$, so

$$
x^2 + y^2 + 1 \ge 0 + 0 + 1 = 1 > 0.
$$

Therefore, $x^2 + y^2 + 1$ can never equal $0$ for any $(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
