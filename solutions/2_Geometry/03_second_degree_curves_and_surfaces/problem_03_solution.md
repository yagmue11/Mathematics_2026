### Exercise 3. Ellipse

For

$$
\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1,
$$

find the semiaxis lengths, the intercepts with the coordinate axes, and make a sketch. Explain along which axis the ellipse is more stretched and how this is visible in the equation.

### Solution

**1. Semiaxis lengths**

Writing the denominators as squares gives

$$
\frac{x^2}{5^2} + \frac{y^2}{3^2} = 1.
$$

Thus the horizontal semiaxis length is $a = 5$, and the vertical semiaxis length is $b = 3$.

**2. Intercepts with the coordinate axes**

- **$x$-intercepts ($y = 0$):**

$$
\frac{x^2}{25} = 1 \implies x = \pm 5,
$$

giving the points $(-5, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$.

- **$y$-intercepts ($x = 0$):**

$$
\frac{y^2}{9} = 1 \implies y = \pm 3,
$$

giving the points $(0, -3)$ and $(0, 3)$.

**3. Sketch and orientation**

- **Sketch description:** A closed oval centered at $(0,0)$, symmetric across both axes, tangent to the bounding rectangle $[-5, 5] \times [-3, 3]$ at the four intercepts $(\pm 5, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 3)$.
- **Direction of greatest stretch:** Because $a = 5 > b = 3$, the ellipse is stretched more along the **$x$-axis**. In the canonical equation, the larger denominator ($25 > 9$) sits beneath $x^2$, which allows $|x|$ to reach $5$ when $y = 0$, whereas $|y|$ only reaches $3$ when $x = 0$.
