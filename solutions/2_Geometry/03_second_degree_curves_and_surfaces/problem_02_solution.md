### Exercise 2. Circle by Completing the Square

Reduce

$$x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3=0$$

to canonical form. Find the center and radius, then substitute two simple points from the circle into both forms of the equation.

### Solution

**1. Completing the square**

Group the $x$-terms and $y$-terms and move the constant to the right-hand side:

$$(x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 4y) = 3.$$

Complete the square for each variable by adding $\left(\frac{-6}{2}\right)^2 = 9$ and $\left(\frac{4}{2}\right)^2 = 4$ to both sides:

$$(x^2 - 6x + 9) + (y^2 + 4y + 4) = 3 + 9 + 4,$$

which gives the canonical form of the circle:

$$(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 16.$$

**2. Center and radius**

Comparing $(x - 3)^2 + (y - (-2))^2 = 4^2$ with $(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 = r^2$:

- **Center:** $C = (3, -2)$,
- **Radius:** $r = \sqrt{16} = 4$.

**3. Verification with two points**

Moving horizontally from the center $(3, -2)$ by distance $r = 4$ gives two simple points on the circle:

$$P_1 = (3 + 4, -2) = (7, -2), \qquad P_2 = (3 - 4, -2) = (-1, -2).$$

- **Point $P_1 = (7, -2)$:**
  - Canonical form: $(7 - 3)^2 + (-2 + 2)^2 = 4^2 + 0^2 = 16$.
  - General form: $7^2 + (-2)^2 - 6(7) + 4(-2) - 3 = 49 + 4 - 42 - 8 - 3 = 0$.

- **Point $P_2 = (-1, -2)$:**
  - Canonical form: $(-1 - 3)^2 + (-2 + 2)^2 = (-4)^2 + 0^2 = 16$.
  - General form: $(-1)^2 + (-2)^2 - 6(-1) + 4(-2) - 3 = 1 + 4 + 6 - 8 - 3 = 0$.

Both points satisfy both forms of the equation.
